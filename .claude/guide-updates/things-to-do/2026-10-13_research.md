# Research: overview of things to do, activities, attractions, and interactive guides in Chattanooga, TN

## 2026-10-13

1. **Tennessee Aquarium** — Chattanooga's flagship attraction; two-building aquarium (River Journey + Ocean Journey) at Ross's Landing on the downtown riverfront. Consistently ranked among the best aquariums in the US. Likely already covered.
2. **Walnut Street Bridge** — One of the longest pedestrian bridges in the world; free walk connecting downtown to the North Shore over the Tennessee River. Signature free activity. Likely already covered.
3. **Lookout Mountain trio (Rock City, Ruby Falls, Incline Railway)** — Rock City's rock gardens and Lover's Leap; Ruby Falls underground waterfall (open since 1929, elevator + half-mile walk); Incline Railway funicular (since 1895, 72.7% grade near the top, ~10 min ride). A short drive south of downtown. Likely already covered.
4. **Hunter Museum of American Art** — Bluff-top museum of American art on the Riverwalk; mix of historic mansion and modern wings. Cultural anchor downtown. Likely covered via the exhibits guide link.
5. **Erlanger Park (new)** — New Minor League Baseball stadium for the Chattanooga Lookouts, opening April 2026 in the walkable Riverfront District. Features the 24,000 sq ft Coca-Cola Pattern Shop, The Powerhouse, a rooftop deck, and the First Bank Club behind home plate. Strong NEW addition for 2026.
6. **The Tivoli Theatre (reopening)** — "Jewel of the South" historic theatre reopening mid-2026 after renovation: new 190-seat cinema and studio, expanded lobby, restaurant, six bar spaces, modernized marquee. NEW/updated for 2026.
7. **Chattanooga Zoo** — 13-acre accredited zoo two miles east of downtown; home to the world's largest indoor red panda exhibit. Good value, family-friendly. Possibly missing.
8. **Chickamauga & Chattanooga National Military Park** — Oldest and largest US military park; Chickamauga Battlefield (~20 min from downtown) has a strong visitor center and self-drive auto tour. Civil War history. Possibly missing.
9. **Tennessee Valley Railroad Museum** — Heritage railroad with steam and diesel excursion trains, including the Missionary Ridge Local. Ties to Chattanooga's rail history. Possibly missing.
10. **Coolidge Park** — North Shore riverfront park with interactive fountains, open lawns, and a restored antique carousel ($1 rides). Free, very family-friendly. Possibly missing.
11. **Tennessee Riverwalk** — 16-mile paved path along the river for walking, biking, and rollerblading, with many access points. Free. Possibly missing.
12. **Ross's Landing & The Passage** — Riverfront plaza with interactive wading pools; The Passage is a Cherokee memorial where water steps down to the river, popular for splashing. Free. Possibly missing.
13. **Reflection Riding Arboretum & Nature Center** — 317 acres at the base of Lookout Mountain: native plants, Cherokee heritage garden, red wolves and raptors, quiet trails. A local favorite, under-touristed. Likely missing — good hidden-gem add.
14. **International Towing & Recovery Museum** — Quirky museum on the history of towing, with vintage wreckers and a Hall of Fame. Chattanooga invented the tow truck. Unusual local gem. Possibly missing.
15. **Classic Arcade Pinball Museum** — Playable museum of vintage pinball and arcade games; part nostalgia, part fun. Rainy-day pick. Possibly missing.
16. **Chattanooga Market** — Sunday open-air market at the First Horizon Pavilion (near the Choo Choo) with local vendors, food, and artists; seasonal (spring–fall). Possibly missing.
17. **Nightfall Concert Series & Riverfront Nights** — Free summer live-music series: Nightfall on Friday nights at Miller Plaza/Miller Park with art market and food trucks; Riverfront Nights on Saturdays at Ross's Landing. Free seasonal entertainment. Possibly missing — ties to live-music guide.
18. **Raccoon Mountain Caverns** — Guided cave walking tours (Crystal Palace) plus wild caving and above-ground activities on Raccoon Mountain west of town. Adventure pick. Possibly missing.
19. **The Waymark Hotel (new)** — Revitalized 1927 bank building in downtown, now a boutique hotel with a subterranean speakeasy called The Vault. NEW for 2026; relevant to visitor/hosting crossover. Possibly missing.
20. **Renaissance Park / "Cardboard Hill"** — North Shore riverfront park with wetland boardwalks and a grassy hill where locals sled on cardboard; near the Walnut Street Bridge. Free, quirky, family-friendly. Possibly missing.
